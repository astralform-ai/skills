# Screening and diligence

Screening decides whether to spend; diligence decides whether to buy. Both are
evidence disciplines, not vibes — every statement is either a falsifiable test
result or an open question with an owner.

## Turn the thesis into tests

A thesis is not investable until it is falsifiable. Rewrite each pillar as a test
with a threshold and a data source, then run it against the materials in hand.

| Thesis pillar (vague) | Falsifiable test | Kills the deal if |
|---|---|---|
| "Mission-critical software" | Gross retention ≥ 92% for three annual cohorts; top-3 customers < 25% of ARR | GRR < 88% or concentration > 35% |
| "Fragmented market, platform opportunity" | Top-5 share < 20%; ≥ 8 credible add-on targets doing > $2m EBITDA | The two largest competitors are PE-backed consolidators already owning the segment |
| "Pricing power" | Realized price per unit up in ≥ 3 of last 4 years without volume loss | Discounting is up and price is flat |
| "Recurring, contracted revenue" | Recurring revenue ≥ 70% of total; median contract term ≥ 12 months | Renewal is at the customer's discretion with no notice period |

**Disqualifiers run before merits.** Apply these first; a deal that trips one is a
hard pass regardless of how good the rest looks — say so and stop, don't write two
pages on the upside of a deal that cannot clear the bar.

- Fund size/X: outside the size band and no path to it through add-ons.
- Sector outside mandate (state the mandate; do not stretch it after the fact).
- Customer concentration the platform cannot survive losing (one customer > 25% of
  revenue, or a contract with a termination-for-convenience right).
- Unresolved regulatory or litigation exposure that caps value regardless of
  operating performance.
- Management unwilling to roll or stay through transition, when that is the
  thesis's key dependency.

## Scorecard — same dimensions every deal

A scorecard is only useful if it is identical across deals; a bespoke scorecard is
a rationalization. Score 1–5 per dimension, weight once, and keep the file.

| Dimension | Weight | What earns a 5 | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thesis fit | 20% | Tests above pass with margin | |
| Revenue quality | 20% | Recurring, low churn, priced, low concentration | |
| Unit economics | 15% | Contribution margin and payback at or above benchmark | |
| Growth durability | 15% | Growth driven by repeatable demand, not one-offs | |
| Management | 15% | Proven, rolling, gaps identified and fillable | |
| Value-creation upside | 10% | ≥ 2 levers with quantified, owned EBITDA impact | |
| Exit optionality | 5% | Multiple strategic and sponsor buyers | |

Verdict vocabulary — use exactly these three, so a partner reading forty screens
sees one scale:

- **Pass** — clears the bar; open diligence with named workstreams.
- **Further diligence** — viable but one or more tests are unproven; list exactly
  what must be seen and by when.
- **Hard pass** — a disqualifier or a failed test that diligence cannot fix. State
  the single reason; do not bury it under the bear case.

Every screen states the thesis it was tested against. A verdict with no thesis
attached is unfalsifiable and worthless.

## Diligence checklist — the question first, the evidence second

Each item exists to answer one question and is closed by one piece of evidence. An
item marked "complete" with no evidence is a lie in a tracker; leave it "in review"
until the evidence exists.

### Commercial

| Item | Question it answers | Evidence that closes it |
|---|---|---|
| Market map | Is the market as big and as growing as claimed? | Bottom-up sizing from disclosed volumes/prices, not an analyst's TAM |
| Win/loss analysis | Why do they win, and against whom? | Last 20 opportunities with outcome, competitor, and reason |
| Customer interviews | Do customers agree with the seller's story? | 8–12 interviews, 2 of them churned, with a written synthesis |
| Pricing review | Can they raise price? | Price list history, realized discounting by segment, escalation clauses |
| Pipeline quality | Is forward revenue real? | CRM extract with stage definitions, conversion by stage, and coverage ratios |
| Concentration | How much does one loss hurt? | ARR by customer, top 20, with contract end dates and renewal terms |

### Financial

| Item | Question it answers | Evidence that closes it |
|---|---|---|
| Quality of earnings | Is EBITDA real and recurring? | Signed QoE report; every adjustment agreed line-by-line with the seller |
| Revenue recognition | When does revenue exist? | Contract samples for each material revenue stream against the policy |
| Working capital | What is normal, and what will day one need? | 24 months of monthly NWC with seasonality and a normalized peg proposal |
| Debt and debt-like items | What else is being assumed? | Payroll deferrals, deferred consideration, customer deposits, leases |
| Capex | What is maintenance vs growth? | Asset register with age/condition; maintenance capex matched to volume growth |
| Tax structure | What is the after-tax cash flow? | Structure paper from counsel, including asset-vs-stock and any NOLs |

### Legal

| Item | Question it answers | Evidence that closes it |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate structure | Do we own what we think we own? | Cap table, subsidiaries, ownership/control opinions |
| Material contracts | Which terms can change the economics? | Full text (not summaries) of top contracts; change-of-control and MFN clauses |
| Litigation | What could take cash out? | Counsel's litigation report with reserve estimates and probability |
| Intellectual property | Is the product defensible? | Assignments from every contributor; freedom-to-operate in the key jurisdiction |
| Employment | Can the team leave? | Employment and non-compete agreements for the top 10 by value created |
| Regulatory | Can they keep operating? | Licences and the correspondence file with the regulator |

### Operational

| Item | Question it answers | Evidence that closes it |
|---|---|---|
| Management assessment | Can they execute the plan? | Aligned role-by-role assessment against the value-creation plan |
| Org and key-person risk | What breaks if one person leaves? | Org chart with criticality, succession, and retention terms |
| Systems and data | Is the reported data trustworthy? | System list with owner and an extraction reprocessing the reported numbers |
| Supply chain | What single points of failure exist? | Vendor list with spend, criticality, and contractual alternatives |
| Facilities and insurance | What is covered and what is exposed? | Policy schedule against the risk register |

### Technical (technology-enabled businesses)

| Item | Question it answers | Evidence that closes it |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture | Can it scale to the plan? | Architecture diagram plus the incident and uptime record |
| Technical debt | What will the next two years cost to fix? | Repo audit with a mid-project cost estimate in engineering weeks |
| Security and privacy | What is the blast radius? | SOC 2 / pen-test report with remediation status; DPA and data-map |
| Roadmap | Is the product bet funded? | Roadmap mapped to headcount and to the customer commitments in contracts |

Sector additions: software → ARR quality and cohorts (see
`{baseDir}/references/unit-economics.md`); healthcare → reimbursement and payor
mix; industrial → equipment condition and environmental remediation; financial
services → regulatory capital and credit quality.

## Red flags — severity and escalation

| Severity | Meaning | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Deal-breaker | Breaks the thesis or caps value regardless of operations | Stop the workstream; notify the deal lead and IC the same day |
| Significant | Changes price, structure, or the plan | Escalate to the deal lead within 48h with a valuation/terms impact |
| Manageable | Fixable with cost, time, or a term | Log with owner and resolution path; track to close |

A red flag without a named severity, owner, and valuation/terms impact is a note,
not a flag. Two patterns deserve suspicion rather than patience: the seller is slow
on a specific request (they may be deciding whether to disclose), and the same
number appears differently in two documents (reconcile before proceeding, never
average them).

## Diligence meeting prep

One page per meeting: objective (the three things you must learn), 15–20 prioritized
questions grouped by topic, benchmarks to reference, the red flags to probe, and
what to request afterwards.

- Lead open-ended; let management talk, then chase specifics. Never "isn't it true
  that…" — that teaches the witness to agree.
- Management presentation: revenue architecture, cohort retention, competition,
  margin bridge, capex vs growth, cost of the plan, and what management would do
  differently.
- Expert call: the market's own view of the target's positioning, the strongest
  competitor, and what an investor should diligence most carefully.
- Customer reference: why they bought, alternatives considered, renewal intent, and
  reaction to a 10–20% price rise.
- End every meeting with: "What have we not asked that we should?" Then write the
  answers down as open items — an unrecorded answer is an unasked question.
